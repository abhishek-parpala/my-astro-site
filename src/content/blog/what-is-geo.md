Traditional SEO optimizes for one thing: showing up in a ranked list of
blue links on a search results page. That model assumes a human is
scrolling through results, comparing snippets, and clicking through.

Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) targets a different surface
entirely — the answer itself. When someone asks ChatGPT, Perplexity, or
Google AI Overviews a question, there's no list of ten links to scroll
through. There's one generated answer, and it either mentions your
brand or it doesn't.

## Why this matters now

More buying research is starting inside AI chat interfaces instead of
traditional search. If a prospective customer asks an AI platform "what's
the best [category] tool for [use case]," being the brand that gets
named in that answer is worth more than ranking #3 for the equivalent
Google query — there's no scrolling required to reach you.

## What actually influences GEO

A few consistent patterns from working across GEO client engagements:

- **Citable, structured content.** AI models tend to pull from pages that
  clearly state facts, comparisons, and definitions rather than pages
  optimized purely for keyword density.
- **Off-page authority.** Being mentioned and linked from sources the
  model already trusts (review sites, forums, industry publications)
  matters more than on-page tricks.
- **Real community discussion.** Reddit and similar community
  discussions show up disproportionately often as sources AI platforms
  cite — genuine, non-manufactured engagement carries real weight.

## Where to start

If you're only going to do one thing, audit what AI platforms currently
say about your brand today. Ask ChatGPT and Perplexity directly what
they know about your product category and see whether you show up at
all. That gap is usually the clearest starting point for a GEO strategy.
